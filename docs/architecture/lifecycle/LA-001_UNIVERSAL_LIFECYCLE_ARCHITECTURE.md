# Purpose

This document defines the Universal Lifecycle Architecture used throughout KoreLumina.

Every organizational asset follows a governed lifecycle.

The lifecycle provides a consistent framework for creation, operation, learning, evolution, and retirement.

Individual architectural domains extend this lifecycle but shall not replace it.

---

# Core Principle

Everything that creates organizational value has a lifecycle.

Examples include:

• capabilities

• knowledge

• evidence

• intelligence

• missions

• experiences

• runtime services

• platform services

• architectural decisions

• governance policies

Lifecycle consistency enables organizational consistency.

# Stage Definitions

## Need

Recognize an organizational opportunity, problem, or strategic objective.

Output:

A validated organizational need.

---

## Discovery

Collect evidence, analyze context, and determine whether an existing solution already satisfies the need.

Output:

Evidence-based understanding.

---

## Definition

Define purpose, scope, ownership, boundaries, dependencies, and success criteria.

Output:

A governed organizational contract.

---

## Design

Design the architecture, workflows, interactions, and operational model.

Output:

A complete design ready for implementation.

---

## Implementation

Realize the design in software, processes, documentation, or organizational structures.

Output:

A functioning capability.

---

## Validation

Verify technical quality, organizational alignment, governance compliance, and expected outcomes.

Output:

Production readiness.

---

## Operation

Deliver value through normal organizational use.

Output:

Operational outcomes and telemetry.

---

## Observation

Continuously collect evidence from operation.

Examples include:

• engineering metrics

• runtime telemetry

• user workflows

• organizational behavior

• mission execution

Output:

Operational evidence.

---

## Learning

Transform evidence into validated knowledge and intelligence.

Output:

Improved organizational understanding.

---

## Evolution

Apply learning to improve the governed asset.

Evolution may affect:

• architecture

• workflows

• experience

• automation

• implementation

The governing identity remains stable.

Output:

An improved version.

---

## Maturity

The governed asset consistently delivers value with predictable quality and established organizational trust.

Output:

Stable organizational capability.

---

## Legacy

The asset continues operating while successors are introduced.

Knowledge, relationships, and history remain available.

Output:

Controlled transition.

---

## Retirement

Retire operational responsibility while preserving organizational memory.

Retirement never destroys:

• evidence

• knowledge

• intelligence

• historical relationships

• governance records

Output:

Permanent organizational memory.

# Governance

Every governed asset shall define:

• owner

• lifecycle stage

• version

• provenance

• dependencies

• consumers

• success metrics

• governance authority

Lifecycle status shall always be observable.

---

# Success Criteria

The Universal Lifecycle succeeds when:

• all architectural domains use a common lifecycle

• lifecycle stages are understandable

• governance is consistent

• organizational learning compounds

• implementations remain replaceable

• organizational knowledge is preserved

---

# Long-Term Outcome

KoreLumina operates through a single, coherent lifecycle architecture that governs every organizational asset.

This consistency reduces architectural complexity, strengthens governance, and enables continuous organizational evolution without sacrificing institutional memory.
